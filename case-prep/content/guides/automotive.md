# Automotive

## Snapshot
- About 80M vehicles sold globally each year. China is the biggest market at ~26M. Toyota sells ~10M a year. The Ford F-Series is the US best-seller at ~650K.
- **The value chain:** OEMs ↔ tier-1/2 suppliers ↔ dealers ↔ lenders ↔ aftermarket, all under safety and emissions regulators.
- Heavy R&D spending is going into EVs, autonomy and connected cars.

## Players
- **OEMs**: Toyota, VW Group, GM, Ford, Hyundai-Kia, Honda, Renault-Nissan-Mitsubishi, plus EV-native companies (Tesla, Rivian, BYD, Nio).
- **Suppliers**: Bosch, Denso, Continental, ZF, Valeo, Magna (brakes, steering, electronics, batteries).
- **Dealers**: ~17K US dealerships. The largest chains (AutoNation ~300 stores, Penske, Lithia) are still a small share of the total, so the market is fragmented.
- **Mobility**: Uber, Lyft, Didi.
- **Auto finance**: OEM captive finance arms plus banks (e.g., Wells Fargo's $25B+ auto loan book).
- **Tech**: Alphabet/Waymo, Apple (CarPlay), Tesla's in-house software.

## Trends
- **Electrification**: still a minority of sales, but the long-run direction is clear.
- **Autonomy**: still early. Robotaxis are running in a few cities. *Update note: the source cites GM's Cruise running 24/7 in SF. Cruise suspended operations after an October 2023 incident, and GM wound it down in late 2024. Waymo is the leading US example now.*
- **Software and connectivity**: infotainment is a battleground. GM is moving away from Apple CarPlay to keep control of the dashboard and its data.
- **Tighter emissions rules** push the industry toward hybrids and EVs.
- **Shared mobility** could reduce car ownership over time.

## Money in
- **New and used vehicle sales**: the largest line. Accounting varies: some OEMs book revenue on the wholesale sale to the dealer, and captive finance income may be reported separately.
- **Financing and leasing**: a significant profit pool for OEM captives and banks.
- **Parts and accessories**: mostly a supplier and aftermarket business. Tesla is an exception, selling add-ons through its app.
- **After-sales**: service, repairs, warranty and insurance. High margin, and it carries **dealers'** profitability.

## Money out
- **Raw materials**: steel, aluminum, rubber, and now battery minerals.
- **Labor**: wages, benefits and legacy pensions (e.g., UAW contracts).
- **R&D**: EV platforms, software, ADAS.
- **Energy and utilities** for plants.
- **Logistics**: inbound parts and outbound vehicles.

## Metrics
| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATP** (average transaction price) | What buyers actually pay after incentives and markups. Compare it with **MSRP** (the list price) |
| **Incentive spend / vehicle** | Discounting pressure (the flip side of ATP vs. MSRP) |
| **Fuel economy** | MPG or L/100km. Feeds regulatory fleet averages |
| **Dealer performance** | Sales per dealer, CSAT, **days of inventory** |
| **Warranty claims / quality** | A proxy for reliability |
| **Supply chain** | Lead times, on-time delivery, inventory levels |

## Case angles
- **Market sizing** (e.g., the US EV pickup segment).
- **Investment**: should we build a battery plant or make this autonomy bet? Look at NPV, strategic fit, and build vs. partner.
- **Operations**: supply-chain resilience (the chip shortage), plant footprint.
- **Dealer vs. direct-to-consumer** distribution.
