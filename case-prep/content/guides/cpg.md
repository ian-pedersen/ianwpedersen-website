# Consumer Packaged Goods (CPG)

## Snapshot
- Everyday products: food, beverages, household and personal care. P&G alone has ~65 brands.
- **Three themes:** it is **acquisitive** (big firms buy growth), **distribution-obsessed** (not on the shelf means no sale) and **marketing-heavy** (P&G spends billions a year on advertising).
- DTC and e-commerce are disrupting the traditional model.

## Players
- **Multinationals**: P&G, Unilever, Nestlé. They have scale advantages in media buying, distribution and R&D.
- **Challenger / startup brands**: Honest Co., Halo Top, RXBAR. Niche, nimble, often DTC.
- **Private label**: Kirkland (Costco, ~$60B), Great Value (Walmart), Amazon Basics. Retailers become competitors.
- **Contract manufacturers**: make products for others.
- Categories behave differently (food vs. beauty vs. household), so ask which category you're in.

## Trends
- **E-commerce and DTC**: Unilever bought Dollar Shave Club for ~$1B, and P&G bought Native. Both deals bought digital capability and a customer relationship.
- **Health and wellness**: natural, clean-label products.
- **Sustainability**: consumer and regulatory pressure (e.g., Nestlé's net-zero-by-2050 pledge), packaging.
- **M&A as a growth engine**: P&G's acquisitions include Gillette, Old Spice, Iams and Native.
- **Personalization**: early stage (e.g., AI-driven skincare).

## Money in (revenue is just product sales, so focus on what *drives* volume and price)
- **Brand strength**, which supports premium pricing.
- **Innovation**, which gives a "new and improved" story and line extensions.
- **Distribution**: shelf presence and placement. Note that DTC is a whole different operating model from selling truckloads to Target.
- **Supply chain reliability**: out-of-stocks are lost revenue (a lesson from COVID).

## Money out
- **COGS ≈ ~50% of revenue**: raw materials (grains, sugar, oils), packaging, and distribution/logistics.
- **Marketing**: roughly 10% of revenue at P&G.
- **R&D**.
- Also **trade spend** (promotions and slotting fees paid to retailers). Not in the source, but a common case lever.

## Metrics
| Retail-channel model | DTC model |
|---|---|
| **On-shelf availability (OSA)**: % of time the product is in stock | **AOV**: average order value |
| **Sell-through by retailer / region** | **CAC**: customer acquisition cost |
| **Share of shelf**: % of shelf space | **CLV / LTV**: lifetime value. Healthy when LTV is well above CAC |

Also know **market share** and **price/mix vs. volume** splits of growth.

## Case angles
- **M&A**: should we buy the hot challenger brand? Look at growth durability, synergies (distribution, procurement) and culture.
- **Cost reduction**: break unit cost into materials, packaging, freight and manufacturing.
- **Growth**: a new health trend, a new channel (DTC, club stores), international expansion.
- **Private label threat**: a competitive response case.

## Self-quiz
1. A brand's revenue is flat. Split the growth into price, mix and volume. What would each one tell you?
2. Why might a multinational buy a DTC brand instead of building one?
3. What is trade spend, and why is it a profit lever?
