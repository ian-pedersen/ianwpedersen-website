# Market archetypes

When the interviewer describes a market, a competitor set or a cost structure, each one implies a story. Use these to make hypotheses fast and to sound fluent when you synthesize.

## Markets

### Commoditized vs. differentiated

| | Commoditized | Differentiated |
|---|---|---|
| Product | Similar across players | Unique value proposition |
| Customers | Price sensitive | Willing to pay a premium |
| Margins | Low | Healthy |
| Wins by | Scale and volume | Staying visibly different |
| Landscape | Consolidated, big budgets | Fragmented, many propositions |

### New vs. mature

- **New markets** grow fast (often double-digit, above GDP) but growth slows. The landscape is unstable, entrants pile in, M&A is common, spending on marketing and operations is heavy, and profits are low or negative.
- **Mature markets** grow near GDP (~2–3%). Established players with strong brands and distribution dominate, competitive pressure is high, and growth means taking share or disrupting with a new value proposition.

### High-end, mass and low-end

- **High-end**: high quality and price, high margins, brand-loyal customers, heavy marketing, fragmented, smaller market.
- **Mass market**: in between.
- **Low-end**: basic and cheap, single-digit margins, price-sensitive customers, focus on cost and distribution, consolidated, grows through M&A, large market.

## Competition

### Consolidated vs. fragmented

- **Consolidated**: 2–3 players hold over half the market, with distribution, brand and budget advantages plus economies of scale. Hard to enter; often mature and commoditized.
- **Fragmented**: no one holds 50%, many small and mid-size firms, easier to enter, roll-up M&A is a natural strategy. Common in location- or taste-driven, high-end, B2B and IP-heavy businesses.

### How companies compete (4Ps)

- **Product**: features and experiences; keep innovating because rivals copy
- **Price**: low prices or financing terms
- **Place**: distribution, high-traffic locations, digital channels
- **Promotion**: advertising, brand awareness, loyalty programs

### Barriers to entry

Capital, technology, brand and loyalty, switching costs, distribution access, regulation, scale.

## Customers

Key purchase criteria and where they dominate:

- **Price**: commoditized and low-end markets
- **Quality**: high-end and differentiated markets
- **Convenience**: subscriptions
- **Brand**: high-visibility products
- **Trust**: finance, healthcare, cybersecurity

## Business cost structures

| | High variable cost | High fixed cost |
|---|---|---|
| Main costs | Materials, components, labor per unit | Capex, rent, equipment, marketing, R&D |
| Operating leverage | Low | High |
| Profit lever | Process efficiency | Volume and scale |
| Examples | Professional services, resellers, mass consumer goods | Telecom, rental businesses, high-end brands, software |
