# Energy

## Snapshot
- About 164K TWh of global energy consumption. The US produces ~12M barrels of oil a day. 191 GW of new solar capacity was added in 2022. HVAC is ~half of household energy use.
- **Geopolitics matters.** Resource-rich countries have leverage, and importers are exposed.
- Extremely capital intensive, with very long asset lives.

## Players
- **Oil & gas majors**: ExxonMobil, Shell, Chevron. They span upstream (exploration and production), midstream (pipelines) and downstream (refining and retail).
- **Renewables developers**: NextEra, Enel, Ørsted.
- **Utilities**: Duke, E.ON, National Grid. They are regulated monopolies for generation, transmission and distribution.
- **Equipment and services**: oilfield (Halliburton, Schlumberger), wind (Vestas, Siemens Gamesa), solar (First Solar, JinkoSolar).
- **Traders**: Vitol, Trafigura, BP trading. They provide liquidity.

## Trends
- **Renewables growth**, now often cost-competitive with fossil fuels.
- **Policy**:
  - The Paris Agreement, aiming for warming well below 2°C.
  - **Feed-in tariffs** guarantee a fixed price to renewable generators.
  - **Renewable portfolio standards** require utilities to source X% renewable power.
- **Shale and fracking** turned the US into a top producer.
- **Storage**: batteries and pumped hydro are needed to handle intermittent supply.
- **Digitization**: sensors, analytics, AI.
- **Future**: decarbonization (including CCS), electrification (EVs, heat pumps, smart grids), energy access, efficiency.

## Money in
- Oil & gas production and sales.
- Coal (power, steel).
- Power generation, transmission and distribution.
- Renewables: equipment manufacturing and installation, plus power sales (often via long-term **PPAs**, or power purchase agreements).
- Energy services and efficiency (smart grid, energy management, consulting).

## Money out
- **Capex**: projects run into billions (e.g., the Barakah nuclear plant, 5.6 GW, ~$24B).
- **Opex**: labor, fuel and maintenance, often in harsh environments like offshore rigs.
- **Tech development**: e.g., new battery chemistries.
- **Regulatory compliance**: emissions controls, safety, taxes.

## Metrics
| Applies to | Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| All | **ROIC** | NOPAT ÷ invested capital. Capital-allocation quality |
| Oil & gas | **Reserve replacement ratio (RRR)** | Reserves added ÷ reserves produced. Above 100% is sustainable |
| Oil & gas | **Production (BOE/day)** | Operating volume |
| Oil & gas | **Lifting / production cost per BOE** | Cost competitiveness |
| Oil & gas | **Revenue per BOE** | Price realization |
| Renewables | **Installed capacity (MW/GW)** | Scale |
| Renewables | **Capacity factor** | Actual output ÷ max possible output. Solar ~20–25%, wind ~35%, nuclear ~90% |
| Renewables | **LCOE** | Lifetime cost ÷ lifetime MWh. The apples-to-apples cost comparison across energy sources |

## Case angles
- **Market sizing** (e.g., rooftop solar in a region).
- **Investment**: NPV of a plant or new technology, which is sensitive to commodity price, capacity factor and discount rate.
- **Competitive response**: a new entrant or technology.
- **Utility strategy**: meeting an RPS target, or deciding between building vs. contracting via PPA.
