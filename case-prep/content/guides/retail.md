# Retail

## Snapshot
- Ranges from corner stores to Amazon (~3B site visits a month).
- Faces constant pressure from changing consumer preferences and e-commerce.
- Examples of scale: Target has ~1,950 stores, Louis Vuitton ~460, Costco membership is ~$120 a year (executive tier), and Dollar Tree's floor is now $1.25.

## Players by model
- **Brick-and-mortar**: the advantage is touching the product and taking it home immediately.
- **Pure-play online**: Amazon, ASOS. Wide selection, but shipping and returns are a cost.
- **Omnichannel**: Target, Nike. Store, web and app all integrated.
- **Discount**: Costco (membership fees are much of its profit), Dollar General. High volume, thin margins.
- **Luxury**: Louis Vuitton, Tiffany. Experience, brand and pricing power.

## Trends
- **E-commerce** (Amazon, Alibaba) forced stores to differentiate.
- **Omnichannel**.
- **Mobile commerce**: Starbucks' app drives 20%+ of its sales.
- **Sustainability and ethical sourcing**.
- **Future**:
  - AI personalization.
  - **Frictionless checkout** (contactless, self-checkout).
  - **Unified commerce**: BOPIS (buy online, pick up in store) and real-time inventory.
  - **AR/VR** try-ons (IKEA furniture placement, Sephora makeup).

## Money in (revenue is merchandise sales, so focus on the drivers)
- **Store revenue = traffic × conversion × AOV.** This is the core retail equation.
- **Consumer spending**: macro-driven (income, jobs, interest rates).
- **E-commerce sales**: web, app, social.
- **Pricing and margin**: markup, dynamic pricing, promotions.
- **Inventory and supply chain**: right product, right place, right time.

## Money out
- **COGS**: purchase cost from suppliers. Directly sets gross margin.
- **Rent and occupancy**: leases, utilities, property tax.
- **Labor**: store staff. Turnover adds cost.
- **Marketing**.
- **Inventory carrying costs**: warehousing, shrink, markdowns on slow movers.
- (For e-commerce: **fulfillment, shipping and returns**.)

## Metrics
| Metric | Formula / meaning |
|---|---|
| **Foot traffic** | Visitors entering. High traffic with low sales means a conversion problem |
| **Conversion rate** | Buyers ÷ visitors |
| **AOV** | Revenue ÷ transactions |
| **Sales per sq ft** | Revenue ÷ selling space. Compares stores across sizes |
| **Inventory turnover** | COGS ÷ average inventory. Higher means leaner |
| *(Also)* **Same-store sales (comps)** | Growth excluding new stores. The key health signal |
| *(Also)* **Gross margin, GMROI** | Profitability of merchandise |

## Case angles
- **Promotion profitability**: incremental volume vs. margin given up vs. cannibalization and pull-forward.
- **Market entry / new store**: catchment demographics, competition, sales per sq ft benchmarks, payback.
- **Cost reduction**: labor scheduling, shrink, store footprint.
- **Online vs. store mix**, fulfillment economics.
