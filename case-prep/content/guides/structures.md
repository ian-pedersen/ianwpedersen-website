# Case structures playbook

How to build a structure, how to check and present it, and the default buckets for each common case type. Use the buckets as a starting point and rename them to fit the prompt.

## Building a structure in three steps

1. **Break the problem into 3–5 first-level drivers.** Go top-down (logical components, often a known framework) or bottom-up (brainstorm ideas, then group them).
2. **Prioritize.** Lead with the driver most likely to answer the question.
3. **Add depth and insight.** Second-level drivers and hypotheses specific to this client.

## The AIM test

| Check | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Answer-focused** | Does it target the client's real goal and question, with a prioritized path to the answer? |
| **Insightful** | Would it work just as well for a different client or industry? If yes, tailor it more. |
| **MECE** | At most 4–5 non-overlapping drivers per level, and every important idea has a home? |

Before structuring: restate the goal in one line, clarify only what changes your structure, and state your assumptions.

## Presenting it

Signpost every first-level driver up front ("three areas: first…, second…, third…"), then walk through the first driver in detail with your hypothesis.

## Four shapes of structure

| Type | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Bucket-based** | 3–4 independent areas to investigate | Most cases: market entry, M&A, PE, investments |
| **Tree-based** | Break the goal into drivers, MECE at each level | Profitability, issue trees with options |
| **Mathematical** | Write the answer as an equation, then explore each term | Revenue targets, sizing, profit |
| **Matrix-based** | Cross two dimensions (e.g. client vs. competitors × position vs. costs) | Comparing players or options |

## Logical frameworks

Use these when the question doesn't fit a standard case type.

| Approach | Best for | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Equation** | Quantitative goals | Profit = (drivers of revenue) − (drivers of cost) |
| **Key questions** | Qualitative yes/no decisions | What would I need to know to decide? |
| **Hypotheses** | Qualitative decisions | "Yes, if A, if B, if C" |
| **Root causes** | Investigating a problem | Causes → causes of causes → testable leaves |
| **Process map** | Operations and business models | Steps in order, losses at each step |
| **From-to** | Reaching a future state | Objective → current state → close the gap |
| **Project plan** | Completing a task | The steps you'd follow running the project |

## Academic frameworks

- **Supply and demand**: price changes, forecasting, markets outside business, capacity vs. demand
- **3Cs**: company and competitors (supply), customers (demand)
- **Porter's five forces**: industry attractiveness (rivalry, entrants, substitutes, supplier and buyer power)
- **4Ps**: product, price, place, promotion

## Default buckets by case type

| Case type | Bucket 1 | Bucket 2 | Bucket 3 | Bucket 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Profitability** | Revenue (price × volume) | Costs (fixed, variable) | The client / unit | The market |
| **Growth** | Revenue analysis | Grow the core | Grow outside the core | The market |
| **Cost reduction** | Cost structure | Client operations | Industry benchmarks | Ideas: need → resources → price |
| **Pricing** | Target segments (if undefined) | Value to customer (ceiling) | Competitors and substitutes | Costs (floor) |
| **Market entry** | Market opportunity | Our potential share | Potential profit | Capabilities and risks |
| **Product launch** | Target segments | Marketing mix (4Ps) | Implementation | Economics and risks |
| **M&A** | Target's market | Standalone value | Synergies | Capabilities and risks |
| **Private equity** | The market | The target | Financials, returns, exit | Risks |
| **Investment** | Impact on revenue | Impact on costs (break-even) | Strategic benefits | Implementation and risks |
| **Competitive threat** | Impact on us | Competitor's opportunity | Response options | |
| **Process** | Map current process | Fix each step | Estimate gains | |

## What goes in the common buckets

### The market
Size, growth rate, competitive landscape (major players, share, consolidated vs. fragmented), typical margins, key trends.

### The client or target
Customer segments, products and services, channels, capabilities, competitive advantages, management.

### Financial analysis
Revenue (price × volume, by segment), costs (fixed vs. variable), investment required, the client's criteria (ROI, payback, IRR), expected profit.

### Risks
- **Market-specific**: demand, competitive response, regulation, macro
- **Client-specific**: operational (capacity, know-how, integration) and financial (funding, cash flow)

### Second-level checklist
Market (size, growth, geographies, segments, channels) · industry (success factors, barriers, disruption) · customers (segments, preferences, purchase decision) · competition (shares, entrants, substitutes) · macro (tech, regulation, unions, economy, politics) · execution (skills, capital, brand, partners).

## Quick ways to split a problem

Internal vs. external · pros vs. cons · revenue vs. cost · financial vs. non-financial · supply vs. demand · make vs. buy · short vs. long term · customer funnel · value chain · segmentation.
