## Q: A grocery chain piloted rooftop solar panels at 20 stores with good results. Should it install them across all 600 stores? Structure it.
@mode recall
@tags investment, structure
1. **The client and the pilot**
   - Store types and locations; how representative were the pilot stores?
   - Pilot setup and results (energy saved, cost, issues)
2. **Financial analysis**
   - Investment required vs. the client's criteria (ROI, payback)
   - Projected savings and any new revenue (selling power back, incentives)
   - Costs to install and maintain
3. **Strategic benefits**
   - Sustainability and brand, customer perception
   - Energy-price hedge, operational resilience
4. **Risks**
   - Market (policy and incentive changes, energy prices)
   - Client (operational rollout, financing)

## Q: Investment decision ("should we invest in X?"): what are the standard buckets?
@tags investment, structure
1. **Impact on revenue**: new revenue, price or volume uplift
2. **Impact on costs**: investment, running costs or savings; does it break even, and when?
3. **Strategic benefits**: brand, customer experience, new opportunities
4. **Implementation and risks**: can we execute it? Market and client risks

For a pilot being scaled, add a first bucket on the pilot itself.

## Q: Comparison (e.g. build in-house vs. outsource): what buckets do you use?
@tags investment, structure
1. **Financial**: capex, opex (fixed and variable), revenue impact
2. **Operations**: time to launch, quality, capacity, capabilities, complexity
3. **Strategic**: new opportunities, access to know-how, customer experience
4. **Risks** for each option

## Q: When scaling a successful pilot, what must you check about the pilot itself?
@tags investment
Whether it is **representative**: were the pilot sites typical (size, location, customer mix)? Do results hold at scale (costs per site, operations, cannibalization)? How long did it run, and were results measured against a control group?
