## Q: Telecom: what are the two sides of the industry?
@tags overview
**Carriers** (services: AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, Comcast) and **equipment makers** (networks: Nokia, Ericsson, Huawei; chips: Qualcomm; devices).

## Q: Telecom: define ARPU and churn.
@tags metric, formula
- **ARPU** = revenue ÷ subscribers, per month
- **Churn** = % of subscribers leaving per month

## Q: How do you approximate subscriber LTV?
@tags metric, formula
**LTV ≈ (ARPU × margin) ÷ churn.** Compare it with CAC.

## Q: Monthly churn drops from 1.0% to 0.8%. What happens to average customer lifetime?
@tags quiz, formula
Lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ churn: **100 months → 125 months, +25%.** Small churn changes compound hugely.

## Q: Telecom: list the revenue streams.
@tags revenue
- Subscriptions: mobile, broadband, TV
- Equipment: devices, routers (and network gear for vendors)
- Value-added services: cloud, streaming bundles, ads
- Enterprise: networking, data centers, unified comms
- Roaming and international

## Q: Telecom: what are the main cost buckets?
@tags cost
- **Network capex**: towers, fiber, data centers
- **Spectrum licenses**: auctions cost tens of billions
- **Marketing / CAC** (~$300 per consumer customer) plus device subsidies
- Labor: field techs, customer care
- Regulatory compliance

## Q: Why do carriers spend so much on spectrum?
@tags quiz, cost
Spectrum is finite and licensed. More of it means more capacity and speed without building as many towers, and it is a barrier to entry.

## Q: What makes 5G hard to monetize?
@tags quiz, trends
Consumers won't pay much more for speed they barely notice, and the enterprise use cases (IoT, autonomous vehicles, smart factories) are still emerging, while the capex is huge.

## Q: Telecom fiber or 5G build-out: what drives payback?
@tags case-angle
**Take rate**, **ARPU uplift**, and **capex per home passed** (plus churn and time to ramp).

## Q: Telecom: what are the typical case angles?
@tags case-angle
- Market entry: new country or fixed-wireless launch
- Investment: 5G or fiber build-out
- Subscriber growth: bundles, family plans, churn reduction
- Competitive response to a price war
