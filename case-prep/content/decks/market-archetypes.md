## Q: Commoditized market: what does it imply?
@tags storytelling, markets
- Products look alike, little differentiation
- Customers are price sensitive → strong price pressure, low margins
- Volume-driven: scale and economies of scale win
- Usually consolidated: a few big players with big marketing budgets, wide distribution and strong awareness

## Q: Differentiated market: what does it imply?
@tags storytelling, markets
- Unique value propositions
- Customers will pay a premium; they buy on more than price
- Healthy margins
- Sales drop if customers stop seeing the difference
- Usually fragmented: many players with different propositions

## Q: New (emerging) market: what does it imply?
@tags storytelling, markets
- High growth (often double-digit, well above GDP) that slows over time
- Attracts entrants; the competitive landscape keeps shifting
- Lots of M&A as big players buy their way in
- Heavy investment in marketing and operations
- Profitability is low or negative

## Q: Mature market: what does it imply?
@tags storytelling, markets
- Low growth, near GDP (~2–3%)
- Established big-name players with strong brands and distribution
- High competitive pressure
- Growth comes from stealing share or disrupting with a new value proposition

## Q: High-end vs. low-end market: compare them.
@tags storytelling, markets
| | High-end | Low-end |
|---|---|---|
| Offer | High quality, high price | Basic, cheap |
| Margins | High | Low (single digit) |
| Customers | Less price sensitive, brand loyal | Price sensitive |
| Competes on | Quality, brand, marketing | Cost, distribution, volume |
| Landscape | Fragmented | Consolidated, grows via M&A |
| Size | Smaller | Large |

## Q: Consolidated competitive landscape: what does it look like?
@tags storytelling, competition
- 2–3 players hold more than 50% of the market
- They have advantages: distribution, brand awareness, big R&D and marketing budgets
- Economies of scale give a cost edge and price flexibility
- Hard to enter or compete; often mature and commoditized

## Q: Fragmented competitive landscape: what does it look like?
@tags storytelling, competition
- No player controls 50%; many small and mid-size firms
- Even leaders can keep gaining share
- Easier to enter
- M&A (roll-ups) is a natural entry and growth strategy
- Typical in location-driven or taste-driven businesses, high-end markets, B2B and IP-heavy sectors

## Q: Name common barriers to entry.
@tags storytelling, competition
Capital requirements, technology, brand and loyalty, switching costs, access to distribution, regulation, and incumbents' economies of scale.

## Q: What are the key purchase criteria, and where does each dominate?
@tags storytelling, customers
- **Price**: commoditized and low-end markets (high price sensitivity, low willingness to pay)
- **Quality**: high-end and differentiated markets
- **Convenience**: subscriptions
- **Brand**: high-visibility products
- **Trust**: finance, healthcare, cybersecurity

## Q: High variable-cost business: what does it imply? Give examples.
@tags storytelling, cost
- Costs tied to each unit: raw materials, components, labor
- Exposed to commodity prices
- **Low operating leverage**: improve processes and efficiency to grow profit
- Examples: professional services (salaries), trading/reselling (COGS), mass consumer products

## Q: High fixed-cost business: what does it imply? Give examples.
@tags storytelling, cost
- Costs don't move with volume: capex, rent, equipment, marketing, R&D
- **High operating leverage**: volume and scale drive profit
- Examples: telecom (capex), rental businesses (assets to maintain), high-end brands (marketing), software (R&D, and marketing if B2C)
