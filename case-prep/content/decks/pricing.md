## Q: A medical-device startup has a new at-home blood test kit. How should it price it? Structure the problem.
@mode recall
@tags pricing, structure
The target customer isn't defined yet, so start there:
1. **Target segments**: patients paying themselves, employers' wellness programs, insurers; their needs and willingness to pay
2. **Value-based**: value to each segment (time saved, convenience, avoided visits) → the price ceiling
3. **Competitor-based**: prices of lab tests and rival kits (direct and indirect); price above or below them?
4. **Cost-based**: unit cost (manufacturing, lab processing, shipping) plus recovering fixed and investment costs → the price floor

## Q: What are the three ways to set a price?
@tags pricing
1. **Cost-based**: variable costs plus a markup big enough to cover fixed costs, the investment and the target margin. Sets the floor.
2. **Competitor-based**: prices of competitors and substitutes. Do we price above or below them?
3. **Value-based**: customers' willingness to pay. Sets the ceiling.

The right price sits between floor and ceiling, based on strategy. If the target segment isn't defined yet, define it first.

## Q: List pricing levers you can brainstorm for revenue growth.
@tags pricing, brainstorm
- Raise or lower price (find the optimal point)
- Tiers: customization, premiumization
- Segment-based pricing (price discrimination)
- Bundles, combos, cross-sell
- Promotions and discounts
- Subscriptions (two-part tariff)
- Dynamic pricing (location, timing)
- Cashback (B2C), rebates (B2B)
