## Q: A regional airline-catering company with 12 kitchens finds its costs are 15% above competitors. They want to close the gap. Structure the problem.
@mode recall
@tags cost-reduction, structure
1. **Cost structure**
   - Fixed: kitchen leases, equipment, salaried staff, overhead
   - Variable: food inputs, packaging, hourly labor, transport
   - Which items are furthest above benchmark?
2. **The client**
   - Business model: customers (airlines), menu range, contract terms
   - Operations: sourcing strategy, kitchen processes, logistics
3. **The industry**
   - Cost benchmarks by line item
   - Trends driving costs (food inflation, fuel, wages)

## Q: What is the three-level tree for generating cost-cutting ideas?
@tags cost-reduction, structure
1. **Reduce the need**: eliminate it entirely (e.g. self-service instead of a call center) or lower the service level (chat-only support)
2. **Meet the need with fewer resources**: cut waste, raise productivity, training
3. **Pay less for the resources**: cheaper alternatives (offshoring, other suppliers), renegotiate

Use it to organize ideas once you know which cost lines matter.

## Q: Cost reduction: what's the first split, and how do you prioritize?
@tags cost-reduction
Split costs into **fixed vs. variable**, then by line item. Prioritize the **biggest** items and the ones **furthest above benchmark**, then ask whether each is a **price** problem (paying too much per unit) or a **volume** problem (using too many units).
