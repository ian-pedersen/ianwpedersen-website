## Q: Name the seven logical ways to structure a question and when each fits.
@tags structure, logical
| Approach | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Equation** | Quantitative goals (profit, revenue, a number) |
| **Key questions** | A qualitative yes/no decision |
| **Hypotheses** | A qualitative decision, phrased as "yes, if…" |
| **Root causes** | Investigating why something went wrong |
| **Process map** | Optimizing an operation or business model |
| **From-to** | Reaching a future state |
| **Project plan** | Completing a task or project successfully |

## Q: Structuring with key questions: how does it work? Give an example.
@tags structure, logical
List the few things you'd need to know to feel comfortable deciding.

*Should we hire this outside sales agency?*
- Have they sold successfully in our industry?
- Will they commit the people and time our targets need?
- Is their fee competitive?

## Q: Structuring with hypotheses: how does it differ from key questions?
@tags structure, logical
Same decision, phrased as conditions that must be true: "We should hire the agency **if** they have industry experience, **if** they can staff the work, and **if** the price is competitive." It makes your point of view explicit from the start.

## Q: Structuring with root causes: how does it work?
@tags structure, logical
Branch on the possible causes of the problem, then on causes of each cause, until each leaf is testable. Example, *why are a courier's deliveries late?* → longer routes than planned · vans leave late (driver not ready, van not loaded) · time lost at drop-off.

## Q: Structuring with a process map: how does it work?
@tags structure, logical
Lay out the steps of the process in order and look for losses at each step. Example, *raise the yield of a hiring process*: attract candidates → screen → select → make offers → accept.

## Q: Structuring with a "from-to": what are the steps?
@tags structure, logical
1. **Define the objective** and the dimensions that describe it (the "to")
2. **Establish the current state** on those dimensions (the "from"), using educated guesses if needed
3. **Close the gap**, dimension by dimension

## Q: Structuring with a project plan: how does it work? Give an example.
@tags structure, logical
Lay out the steps you'd follow if you were running the project. *How should a software firm compete for a large government contract?* → understand the process and requirements → learn from past winners → identify our advantages → prepare the bid.

## Q: A regional bank's online loan applications rose 20% last year, but completed loans fell. Why? Structure it.
@mode recall
@tags structure, logical, root-cause
Root causes, following the path from application to completed loan:
1. **Applicant quality**: weaker credit, incomes not keeping up with prices, less committed applicants
2. **Our process**: too many steps (drop-offs), slow decisions, poor follow-through after an offer
3. **Our approval rules**: tighter criteria or risk models
4. **Our offer vs. competitors**: rates, features, brand

## Q: A national park service wants to know why few people use its new online booking system. What would you look into?
@mode recall
@tags structure, logical, root-cause
Supply / demand / intermediation root causes:
1. **Supply**: too few bookable slots or sites, limited inventory online
2. **Demand**: visitors don't know about it, don't need to book, or don't trust it
3. **Intermediation**: tour operators and visitor centers don't promote or use it
4. **The product itself**: hard to use, bugs, poor mobile experience

## Q: A mid-size city wants to become a top destination for remote workers within five years. What does it need to do?
@mode recall
@tags structure, logical, from-to
From-to:
1. **Define the target**: which remote workers (tech, creatives, families), and what they need
2. **Assess the current state**: cost of living, internet, housing, co-working, lifestyle, transport
3. **Close the gaps**: incentives and visas, housing supply, co-working and community, connectivity, marketing the city
