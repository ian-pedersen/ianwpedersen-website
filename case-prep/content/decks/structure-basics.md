## Q: What are the four types of case structure?
@tags structure
1. **Bucket-based**: 3–4 independent areas to investigate (e.g. market / client / economics / risks)
2. **Tree-based**: break the goal into drivers, MECE at each level (e.g. profit → revenue and cost)
3. **Mathematical**: write the answer as an equation and investigate each term
4. **Matrix-based**: two dimensions crossed (e.g. client vs. competitors × market position vs. costs)

## Q: When does a mathematical (equation) structure work best? Give an example.
@tags structure
For quantitative questions, when the goal is a number you can decompose (profit, revenue, a target). Example for a retail chain's profit:

**Profit = (# stores × volume per store × average price) − costs**

Then attach drivers to each term: new stores (geographic expansion), volume (market growth, share, promotions), price (pricing strategy), costs (structure, reduction areas).

## Q: Draw the classic profitability tree.
@tags structure, profitability
- **Profit improvement**
  - **Revenue growth**
    - Price: price trends, promotions and discounts
    - Volume: product lines, customer segments, market growth, competition
  - **Cost reduction**
    - Fixed: overhead, rent, marketing
    - Variable: labor, inputs, packaging, transportation

## Q: What does an issue tree look like for "how can the client increase profits?"
@tags structure
Branch on the **options**, then list what to check under each:
- Cut costs → cost structure, reduction areas
- New customer segments → current segments, most attractive new ones (e.g. premium, B2B)
- Grow share in current markets → current revenue, product lines, competition, growth
- Expand geographically → current footprint, attractive international markets

## Q: List quick ways to split any problem into two or three MECE halves.
@tags structure, brainstorm
- Internal vs. external
- Pros vs. cons
- Increase revenue vs. decrease costs
- Financial vs. non-financial
- Supply vs. demand
- Make vs. buy
- Benefits to party X vs. party Y, plus risks
- Short term vs. long term
- Financial, environmental, social and technical (triple bottom line plus)

## Q: Name three process frameworks you can use to structure a brainstorm.
@tags structure, brainstorm
- **Customer funnel**: awareness → conversion → repurchase
- **Value chain**: raw materials → manufacturing → distribution → point of sale → marketing
- **Segmentation**: age, geography, income, gender, preferences, channel (online vs. store), product, store-to-store

## Q: Checklist of second-level drivers: what sits under market, industry, customers, competition, macro and execution?
@tags structure, brainstorm
- **Market**: size and growth, geographies, profitability, segments, channels
- **Industry**: key success factors, barriers to entry, tech disruption
- **Customers**: segments, preferences, how they make the purchase decision
- **Competition**: market shares, new entrants, substitutes
- **Macro**: technology, regulation, unions, economy, politics
- **Execution**: skills, capital, brand, partners

## Q: Where can you get information to test a hypothesis in a case?
@tags brainstorm
Competitor benchmarks, expert interviews, customer surveys, news articles, equity research, trade journals, and the client's own data.

## Q: What do almost all case structures have in common?
@tags structure
Usually some version of: **the market**, **the client** (or target), **the economics** (revenue, cost, investment, returns), and **risks**. Tailor the bucket names and the bullets under them to the prompt.
