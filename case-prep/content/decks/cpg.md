## Q: CPG: what are the three themes of the industry?
@tags overview
1. **Acquisitive**: big firms buy growth
2. **Distribution-obsessed**: not on the shelf means no sale
3. **Marketing-heavy**: billions a year on advertising

## Q: CPG: who are the player types?
@tags players
- Multinationals (P&G, Unilever, Nestlé): scale in media, distribution, R&D
- Challenger / startup brands: niche, nimble, often DTC
- Private label (Kirkland, Great Value): retailers become competitors
- Contract manufacturers

## Q: CPG: roughly how big is COGS, and what's in it?
@tags cost
**~50% of revenue**: raw materials (grains, sugar, oils), packaging, and distribution/logistics. Marketing is roughly another ~10%.

## Q: What is trade spend, and why is it a profit lever?
@tags cost, quiz
Payments to retailers: promotions, temporary price cuts, slotting fees and displays. It is often one of the largest costs after COGS, and much of it buys volume that would have happened anyway, so tightening it can lift margin.

## Q: CPG: which metrics matter in the retail channel vs. DTC?
@tags metric
| Retail channel | DTC |
|---|---|
| On-shelf availability (OSA) | AOV |
| Sell-through by retailer / region | CAC |
| Share of shelf | LTV (healthy when well above CAC) |

## Q: What drives CPG revenue beyond "product sales"?
@tags revenue
- Brand strength, which supports premium price
- Innovation: new and improved, line extensions
- Distribution: shelf presence and placement
- Supply chain reliability: out-of-stocks are lost revenue

## Q: A brand's revenue is flat. Split it into price, mix and volume. What would each tell you?
@tags quiz, case-angle
- **Price** up, volume down: pricing power is fading or elasticity is high
- **Mix** shifting to cheaper SKUs: consumers trading down
- **Volume** down: lost distribution, share loss or category decline

Flat totals can hide offsetting moves.

## Q: Why might a multinational buy a DTC brand instead of building one?
@tags quiz, case-angle
- Speed and proven product-market fit
- Digital capability and a direct customer relationship (data)
- An authentic brand that's hard to build inside a big company
- Examples: Unilever–Dollar Shave Club, P&G–Native

## Q: CPG: what are the typical case angles?
@tags case-angle
- M&A: buy the hot challenger? Growth durability, synergies, culture
- Cost reduction: materials, packaging, freight, manufacturing
- Growth: health trends, new channels (DTC, club), international
- Private label threat: competitive response
