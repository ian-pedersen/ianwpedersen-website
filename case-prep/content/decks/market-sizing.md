## Q: How do you approach a market sizing?
@tags market-sizing
1. Clarify the market (units or $, geography, year)
2. Pick an approach: top-down or bottom-up
3. Segment where behavior differs
4. Round numbers, state assumptions, sanity-check the answer

## Q: Top-down vs. bottom-up sizing: how do they differ, and which is preferred?
@tags market-sizing
- **Top-down**: break a large number (population, total market) into pieces. Gets the order of magnitude right but feels abstract. **Usually preferred.**
- **Bottom-up**: extrapolate from something small and observable (one store, one town). Very tangible, but small errors get multiplied. **Great for sanity checks.**

## Q: What is the replacement method for sizing annual sales of a durable good?
@tags market-sizing
**Annual sales = units sold to replace the current stock + units sold to grow the stock.**
Replacement ≈ installed base ÷ product lifetime.

Example: 280M cars on the road ÷ ~15-year life ≈ 19M a year to replace, plus any growth in the fleet.

## Q: How does segmentation sharpen a market sizing? Give an example.
@tags market-sizing
Split the population where behavior differs, then take a weighted average.

Cars per US household: 10% have 0, 30% have 1, 50% have 2, 10% have 3 → 0 + 0.3 + 1.0 + 0.3 = **1.6 cars per household**.
