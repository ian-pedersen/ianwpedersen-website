## Q: A regional dairy cooperative's operating margin has dropped from 9% to 4% in two years. They want to know why and how to restore it. Structure the problem.
@mode recall
@tags profitability, structure
1. **Financial analysis**
   - Revenue: price trends, volume, mix by product (milk, cheese, yogurt) and channel
   - Costs: fixed vs. variable; which items grew fastest
2. **The client**
   - Product portfolio and customer segments (grocers, food service, export)
   - Operations: plants, sourcing, logistics
3. **The market**
   - Growth rate, competitors and their margins
   - Trends: input costs, private label, plant-based substitutes

Alternative: split financial analysis into separate **Revenue** and **Cost** buckets.

## Q: A hospital chain's outpatient imaging unit has become unprofitable. Lay out your structure.
@mode recall
@tags profitability, structure
1. **Revenue**
   - Price: reimbursement rates by payer, contract changes
   - Volume: scans by type (MRI, CT, X-ray), referrals, capacity use
2. **Costs**
   - Fixed: equipment depreciation, leases, salaried radiologists
   - Variable: technicians, contrast agents, consumables
3. **The unit vs. benchmarks**
   - Service mix, patient segments, utilization per machine
4. **The market**
   - Growth, competing standalone centers, typical margins, regulation

## Q: A ski resort in Colorado wants to raise its profits. Structure it.
@mode recall
@tags profitability, structure
1. **Increase revenue**
   - More visitors: is volume limited by **capacity** (lifts, parking, lodging, operating days) or by **demand** (promotions, new channels, season passes, loyalty)?
   - More spend per visitor: price (dynamic lift pricing), add-ons (lessons, rentals, dining, VIP lift lines)
2. **Decrease costs**
   - Lower service level where guests won't notice (fewer lifts midweek, shorter hours)
   - Do the same with less (cut waste, staff to demand)
   - Pay less for inputs (renegotiate suppliers, energy for snowmaking)

## Q: A specialty travel company sells 40 different tour packages, and its profit fell after a weak season. Structure the turnaround.
@mode recall
@tags profitability, structure
1. **Fix the mix first**: cut capacity on the least profitable packages and shift it to the most profitable
2. **Raise profit across all packages**: demand, pricing, costs (overhead; package costs via fewer extras, renegotiated or consolidated suppliers)
3. **Invest in new profitable products**: closer or cheaper destinations, niche trips (adventure, sports), options less exposed to whatever hurt this season

## Q: In a profitability case, when should you not start with the standard revenue/cost tree?
@tags profitability, structure
When the client sells **many products with very different margins**: profit is driven by the **mix**, so start by shifting volume toward the most profitable products, then look at price and cost.

Also tailor "volume" to the business: for capacity-bound businesses (parks, hotels, airlines), ask whether volume is limited by **supply (capacity)** or by **demand**.

## Q: What is the first split in any profitability case?
@tags profitability
**Profit = Revenue − Costs.** Then: revenue = price × volume (by segment / product), costs = fixed + variable. Find which piece changed vs. history and vs. competitors before brainstorming fixes.

## Q: Profitability: which questions isolate the problem fast?
@tags profitability
- Is it a revenue problem, a cost problem, or both?
- Is it the whole company or one product, region or segment?
- Is it us only, or the whole industry (check competitors)?
- When did it start, and what changed then?

## Q: Profitability: what does the market bucket cover?
@tags profitability
Market growth rate, major competitors and share, typical profit margins, and industry trends (input costs, substitutes, regulation, new entrants).
