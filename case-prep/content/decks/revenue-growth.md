## Q: A chain of 400 pet-grooming salons wants to grow revenue 25% in three years. Structure the problem.
@mode recall
@tags revenue-growth, structure
1. **Revenue analysis**
   - Volume: visits per salon, number of salons
   - Price: pricing strategy, mix of services
   - Historical trends by service and region
2. **The client**
   - Key customer groups (dog vs. cat, premium vs. budget)
   - Range of services and add-ons (retail, boarding, memberships)
3. **The market**
   - Growth rate, competitive landscape (big chains, independents, mobile groomers)

Then brainstorm levers: more salons, more visits, higher price, new services, new segments.

## Q: How do you structure "how do we grow this business?"
@tags revenue-growth, structure
**Revenue = volume × price**, then split the options:
1. **Grow the core**
   - Win more in current segments: better products, better marketing, sharper prices
   - Shift toward the fastest-growing segments
2. **Grow outside the core**
   - Sell new products to existing clients (no new acquisition cost)
   - Use our capabilities to enter new businesses, segments or geographies
   - Inorganic: acquisitions, partnerships, JVs

Example for a carmaker: core = better models, low-cost line, Asia; outside = insurance, maintenance plans, leasing.

## Q: Revenue growth target cases: what's the first math check?
@tags revenue-growth, math
Size the gap: target revenue − expected revenue at the current growth rate. Then test whether each lever can plausibly close its share of the gap, and add them up.
