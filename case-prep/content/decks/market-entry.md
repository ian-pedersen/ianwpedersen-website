## Q: A mid-size US coffee roaster with $80M in sales wants to launch a line of ready-to-drink canned cold brew. Should they? Structure it.
@mode recall
@tags market-entry, structure
1. **The market (RTD coffee)**
   - Size and growth rate
   - Competitive landscape: major brands, private label, share
   - Typical margins, key trends
2. **The client's business model**
   - Target customer segments
   - Planned product and pricing
   - Channels: grocery, convenience, online; marketing plan
   - Capabilities: canning, cold chain, distribution
3. **Financial analysis**
   - Investment required and the client's criteria (ROI, payback)
   - Expected profit: projected revenue and costs, by product and segment
4. **Risks**
   - Market risks (competitive response, demand)
   - Client risks: operational (capacity, know-how), financial (cash)

## Q: A European e-bike maker is considering entering the US market. Lay out your structure.
@mode recall
@tags market-entry, structure
1. **US e-bike market**: size, growth, segments (commuter, cargo, leisure), key trends, regulation and tariffs
2. **Competitive landscape**: major players, share, differentiation, typical margins
3. **Client fit**: products that fit US demand, brand, capabilities, entry mode (organic, partner, acquire)
4. **Financials**: investment, expected revenue by segment, costs, success metrics, payback
5. **Risks**: market-specific and client-specific (operational, financial)

## Q: Market entry: what are the four standard buckets?
@tags market-entry
1. **Market opportunity**: size, growth, margins, trends
2. **Our potential share**: competitors, customer needs, our offering and business model (segments, channels)
3. **Potential profit**: investment, revenue, running costs; ROI and payback vs. the client's criteria
4. **Capabilities and risks**: can we execute? Market-specific and client-specific risks (operational, financial)

Sources differ slightly: one splits "market" and "business model" instead of "opportunity" and "share". Same content.

## Q: How do you structure "how should we launch a new product?"
@tags market-entry, product-launch, structure
1. **Choose target segments**: size and growth, competition, customer needs
2. **Define the marketing strategy (4Ps)**: product, price, place (distribution), promotion
3. **Plan implementation**: product design, production, marketing and sales, logistics, after-sales care

## Q: A sportswear brand wants to launch its first smartwatch. How should it go about it?
@mode recall
@tags market-entry, product-launch, structure
1. **Target segments**: runners, gym-goers, casual wellness users; size, growth, competition (Apple, Garmin), unmet needs
2. **Marketing mix**: features vs. rivals, price point, channels (own stores, DTC, retailers), promotion (athletes, existing app users)
3. **Implementation**: design partner or in-house, manufacturing, launch marketing, logistics, support and returns
4. **Economics and risks**: investment, break-even volume, brand risk if quality disappoints

## Q: What are the ways to enter a new market?
@tags market-entry
- **Build** organically (greenfield)
- **Partner** or license
- **Joint venture**: share cost, risk and resources
- **Acquire** an existing player

Trade-off: speed and control vs. cost and risk.

## Q: Market entry: list the factors to consider beyond the market itself.
@tags market-entry, brainstorm
- Regulations and logistics
- Customer study: target groups, needs, willingness to pay
- Business model: marketing strategy, promotions, channels, pricing, offerings
- Operations: timeline, complexity, bottlenecks (production or logistics capacity), capability gaps
- Financials: projected profitability and cash flow, investment vs. criteria, scenarios (poor / base / best)
